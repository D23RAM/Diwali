Discover Diwali

Light over darkness

Diwali (Divali, Deepavali) means "row of lights". Across traditions it marks the victory of light over darkness and of knowledge over ignorance. Homes are cleaned, diyas are lit and families gather.

Shri Ram returns

In the Ramayana tradition, Ram, Sita and Lakshman return to Ayodhya after 14 years of exile and the defeat of Ravana. The city lights lamps to welcome them home.

Maa Lakshmi

Goddess of prosperity and well-being, welcomed by many families on Diwali night with a clean home, diyas and puja.

Maa Saraswati

Goddess of knowledge, music and learning. She stands for the second light Diwali celebrates: wisdom. How she is honoured around Diwali varies by region and family.

Not one Diwali, many

North India

Centred on Ram's return to Ayodhya, with rows of diyas and Lakshmi-Ganesh puja.

Bengal and Odisha

Many families worship Maa Kali on the new-moon night of Diwali.

Gujarat

The days after Diwali begin a new year in the Vikram Samvat calendar.

South India

Often observed as Naraka Chaturdashi, with an early-morning oil bath and lamps at dawn.

Practices differ by region, family and community. Jains and Sikhs also mark these days with their own meanings. Corrections from elders and priests are welcome.

Divali in Suriname

  1. 1863

    Slavery is abolished in Suriname, creating demand for new labour.

  2. 1873

    The ship Lalla Rookh brings the first Indian contract labourers to Paramaribo (5 June).

  3. 1873–1916

    Tens of thousands of Indian contract workers arrive. Verify exact numbers and end date.

  4. After contracts

    Many stay, build farms, families, mandirs and communities, and shape Sarnámi Hindustani. Verify details with historians.

  5. Today

    Families light diyas, hold puja, visit mandirs and share sweets. Local customs to be confirmed with community elders.
